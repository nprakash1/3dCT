## Slide 1 — The Question

**[ON SLIDE]**
- Title: **"Is it better, stable, or worse?"** — reading change in CT over time
- Given a patient's **PRIOR** and **CURRENT** CT, predict per-finding progression:
  **worsened / stable / improved**
- Visual: two CT slices with an arrow between them

**[NOTES]**
- Follow-up imaging is about *change*, not detection. The clinical question in
  oncology/ICU is "how is this evolving?" — tumor response, effusion resolving,
  infection spreading.
- Radiologists answer by comparing prior vs current slice-by-slice; it's slow,
  expert work, and it doesn't scale.
- This is a *relational* task between two 3D volumes, not single-image pattern
  recognition — which is what makes it fundamentally harder for AI.
- Framing: the CT analogue of the CheXTemporal problem for chest X-ray, but 3D,
  volumetric, with far more anatomy and field-of-view variability.
- Thesis: a foundation model gives us great "eyes," but reading *direction of
  change* exposes a specific, measurable weakness — I'll show what it is and how to
  fix it.

## Slide 2 — Dataset & Setup

**[ON SLIDE]**
- CT-RATE → repeat-patient pairs → **600 before/after pairs**
- Labels: MedGemma reads paired reports → per-finding {worsened/stable/improved};
  keep **"explicit"** only
- **Patient-level** train/val/test split; classes ~balanced
- Backbone: **MERLIN** (frozen) — 3D image encoder + Clinical-Longformer text encoder
- Trick: encode once, **cache fingerprints** → experiments run in seconds

**[NOTES]**
- CT-RATE: large public chest-CT set; we mined patients scanned more than once to
  build prior/current pairs.
- Ground truth: an LLM (MedGemma) reads both reports and extracts each finding's
  change; we keep only labels where the report *explicitly* states change (our
  "gold" tier) to reduce noise.
- Splits are by *patient*, so we never test on a patient seen in training — no leakage.
- MERLIN turns each scan into a 512-d fingerprint and each sentence into a 512-d
  fingerprint in a *shared* space (image↔text comparable). We freeze it — it's our
  fixed, trusted encoder.
- Efficiency: because it's frozen, we ran every scan through MERLIN once and cached
  the fingerprints to GCS (~1 MB each). All downstream training runs on cached
  tensors — seconds on a laptop, no GPU.
- Honest caveat: the zero-shot prompts currently cover **18 findings**, so the scored
  test set is **n≈252** (about a third of gold); per-disease numbers are indicative,
  not final.

## Slide 3 — Zero-shot vs Transformer (results)

**[ON SLIDE]**
- Table:

  | Method | macro-F1 | acc |
  |---|---|---|
  | chance / always-"worsened" | 0.33 / 0.21 | — |
  | **Zero-shot** (subtract + cosine) | **0.352** | 0.365 |
  | **Transformer** (trained) | **0.398** | 0.401 |

- Two mini confusion matrices side by side (naive vs trained)
- Caption: *Trained wins — but by rebalancing, not by solving direction.*

**[NOTES]**
- **Zero-shot:** subtract the two fingerprints (current − prior), match that
  difference to "worsened/stable/improved." No training. 0.352 — barely above
  chance; it almost never says "improved," dumping cases into worsened/stable.
- **Transformer:** a small trainable module (told which scan is before/after) learns
  a "change fingerprint," matched to the same prompts. 0.398 — best, and it predicts
  all three classes (improved F1 0.29→0.43).
- **Read the confusion matrices:** naive defaults toward worsened/stable; trained
  over-predicts "improved" — including **46 truly-worsened called improved**. Both
  make the *same* error: they detect *that* something changed but get the *direction*
  wrong.
- So the +0.05 macro-F1 gain is real but shallow: training mostly shifted the
  decision boundary (aided by a learnable temperature that magnifies tiny
  differences), not a genuine grasp of direction.
- Punchline into next slide: "Why can't either model tell worsening from improving?
  The answer isn't in the images — it's in the text."

## Slide 4 — The Cosine / Text-Separability Issue (the "why")

**[ON SLIDE]**
- **0.93** — cosine between "worsened," "stable," "improved" prompts
- **0.94** — "worsened" vs "improved" (should be opposites!)
- same-finding/different-direction **0.93** ≫ different-finding/same-direction **0.65**
- Caption: *MERLIN's text encoder is direction-blind: it encodes the disease, not the change.*

**[NOTES]**
- We tested the *text side alone*: how distinct are the three target phrases inside
  MERLIN? ~93% identical. "Worsened" vs "improved," which should be opposites, are
  94% identical — there is **no direction axis**.
- The space is organized by *disease* (0.93 within a finding) far more than by
  *direction* (0.65 across findings). MERLIN cares about *which finding*, barely
  about *which way it changed*.
- This is a *ceiling*: any method deciding by cosine-to-prompts is choosing among
  three near-identical targets, exploiting only ~7% of separation — exactly why the
  trained model needs a huge temperature and still confuses direction.
- This single diagnostic *predicted* the confusion-matrix behavior on the previous
  slide. That's the intellectual core: we didn't just get a mediocre number, we
  explained it and localized the bottleneck to the frozen text encoder + global
  pooling (which also discards *where* change happened).
- Transition: "So the fix must (a) manufacture a direction signal and (b) stop
  throwing away location. That's the new architecture."

## Slide 5 — New Architecture (TempA-VLP: static + dynamic)

**[ON SLIDE]**
- Diagram C (two-path static/dynamic design)
- Two "games":
  - **Static:** current image `vs` ↔ "now" sentences `ts` → aligns *disease identity*
  - **Dynamic:** change vector `vd` ↔ "change" sentences `td` → aligns *temporal/direction*
- Upgrades: **antisymmetric diff** `d = g(c,p) − g(p,c)` (⇒ "stable" = 0);
  **cross-exam encoder** (keeps spatial tokens); **LLM report splitter**;
  partial **unfreezing / LoRA** of encoders
- `L_total = α·L_dynamic + β·L_static`

**[NOTES]**
- Move from one global fingerprint to **spatial tokens** (MERLIN layer4) fed to a
  trainable **cross-exam encoder** that attends across prior and current — restoring
  *location* and fixing the second ceiling.
- **Two-path decomposition** (answers "why static + dynamic"):
  - *Static path* aligns the current image with "now" sentences — easy, and keeps the
    model anchored to *disease identity*, so the change-path doesn't relearn what a
    finding is.
  - *Dynamic path* aligns the **change vector** `vd` with "change" sentences `td` —
    where direction lives. Supervising against real *comparison* language ("interval
    increase in…") gives the directional signal the templates lacked.
  - The static/dynamic split comes from an **LLM splitter** on the current report (CT
    has no scene-graph like chest X-ray's ImaGenome).
- **Antisymmetric difference** `d = g(c,p) − g(p,c)`: swapping order flips the sign,
  so "no change" → zero vector — "stable" becomes *representable*, and
  worsened/improved become *opposites by design*. Directly attacks the 0.94 problem.
- **Freeze vs unfreeze (the "maybe?"):**
  - Keep the **image encoder frozen** (or LoRA the last block only) — it's expensive
    and already good at anatomy; full fine-tune on 600 pairs would overfit.
  - Slide 4 is the argument for touching the **text encoder**: because it's
    direction-blind, add **trainable projection heads** on both sides at minimum, and
    consider **LoRA on the text encoder** so it learns to spread "increased/decreased"
    apart — *without* full fine-tuning (catastrophic forgetting risk on a small set).
  - Staged recipe: (1) train projection heads + cross-exam encoder, everything else
    frozen; (2) if direction still collapses, enable text-encoder LoRA; (3) optionally
    add a text-side regularizer pushing "increased"↔"decreased" toward antipodality.
- Difference from vanilla TempA-VLP: their `vd` is a raw CLS token (weak on "stable");
  ours is the antisymmetric diff; their backbone is 2D ViT patches, ours is 3D CT
  MERLIN tokens; report split via LLM.

## Slide 6 — Roadmap / What this buys us (closer)

**[ON SLIDE]**
- ✅ Built: cached pipeline, zero-shot floor, trained baseline, separability diagnostic
- 🔜 Next: antisymmetric cross-exam model + static/dynamic InfoNCE; real-report
  supervision; expand prompt bank
- Metric to watch: **worsened↔improved "sign accuracy"** (isolates the direction fix)
- Impact: progression-triage assistant + a clear map of *why* temporal CT is hard

**[NOTES]**
- We already have the full frozen-backbone pipeline, two baselines, and — most
  importantly — a *diagnosis*.
- The new architecture is a targeted response to that diagnosis, not a shot in the
  dark: antisymmetry for direction, spatial tokens for location, static path for
  stability, dynamic path + real language for temporal semantics.
- Success criterion: don't just watch macro-F1 — watch a **sign-accuracy** metric
  (of the cases where change is correctly detected, how often is the direction
  right). That number is what the whole redesign is meant to move.
- Close on impact: a scalable progression-triage tool, and a contribution on the
  limits of foundation-model text encoders for temporal reasoning.
